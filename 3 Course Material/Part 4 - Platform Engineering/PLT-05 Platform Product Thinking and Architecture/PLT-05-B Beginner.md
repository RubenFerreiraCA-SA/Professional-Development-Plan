## MODULE PLT-05-B — PLATFORM PRODUCT THINKING AND ARCHITECTURE — BEGINNER

### 1. Topic and Scope

**In scope for this level:** Foundations of platform thinking. You explain what an internal developer platform is, why a platform has customers, how to write personas and jobs-to-be-done in the "When, I want, so I can" form, what a one-page product brief contains, and how to choose metrics that reflect user outcomes rather than platform activity.

**Out of scope:** Service-boundary and ownership design (Intermediate), integrated outcome matrices and architecture decisions (Professional), organisational strategy and build-versus-buy analysis (Expert), and the Harbour persona-writing step (Project Document).

**Self-assessment fit:** Choose this level in [2.2 Topic Self Assessment](../../../2%20PDP%20Development/2.2%20Topic%20Self%20Assessment.md#plt-05--platform-product-thinking-and-architecture) when this matches your current ability: *Explain what an internal developer platform is and who it serves.* Typical starting point: You have built or used internal tools but have never framed them as a product with users.

### 2. Learning Objectives

1. Define an internal developer platform and distinguish it from a toolbox, a portal and a ticket queue.
2. Write three jobs-to-be-done statements for Harbour developers in the form "When (situation), I want (motivation), so I can (outcome)".
3. Create two realistic personas you could actually recruit for an interview and state the top developer problem each one faces.
4. Draft a one-page product brief naming users, problem, offering, non-goals and one outcome metric, and explain why "number of templates published" is a weak metric.
5. Prepare the evidence needed for **PRJ-085**: personas and a ranked list of top developer problems for the independent training platform, each tied to a source or an interview plan.

### 4. Knowledge Assessment

**Task:** Write a one-page product brief for an imaginary internal platform, naming two real people you could ask for feedback. Include three jobs-to-be-done, one non-goal and an outcome metric a user would recognise.

**Pass criteria:** The brief names a specific user and problem; jobs-to-be-done follow the form; the non-goal is genuine rather than trivial; the metric measures an outcome; interview candidates are real roles, not invented quotes.

Record the result in your Learning Progress Record. Passing the knowledge assessment does **not** by itself complete the module — the project assignment below is also required for a project-linked pathway.
