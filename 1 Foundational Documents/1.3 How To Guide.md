# Programme Guide

Use this framework to build **your own** Platform Engineering PDP. The [Overview](1.1%20Programme%20Overview.md) explains the destination; the [Course Outline](1.2%20Course%20Outline.md) shows the sequence. This guide explains what to do with them.

## 1. Personalise the plan

Before starting, decide:
- **Starting point:** Which skills do you already have, and where are your gaps?
- **Outcome:** Which Platform Engineering capabilities or specialisations matter most to you?
- **Capacity:** How many hours can you study consistently? Plan around the **estimated hours for each module**, adjusting for your experience and capacity.
- **Timing:** Choose your own start date, breaks and milestones. Estimated study hours are a guide, not a fixed calendar schedule.
- **Evidence:** Where will you store lab work, code and notes?

Use the [PDP templates folder](../2%20PDP%20templates/) when templates become available, or begin with your own tracker.

## 2. Follow the learning cycle

For each learning activity:

1. **Learn** the concepts and read the prescribed materials.
2. **Build** the lab or practical deliverable.
3. **Verify** that it works, including failures and recovery where relevant.
4. **Record** a link to the result, plus what you learned.

Aim to complete practical work, not just read about it. If a topic requires more time, adjust your plan rather than skipping the lab.

## 3. Build towards the Final Project

The recommended [Final Project](1.4%20Final%20Project.md) is a **generic Internal Developer Platform** that connects the four disciplines. Adapt or replace it with equivalent practical work if your PDP calls for something different:

| Stage | Project contribution |
| --- | --- |
| DevOps | Repository, containerised app, CI/CD, Terraform, GitOps and monitoring |
| Cloud | Secure infrastructure, IAM, networking, cost controls and resilience |
| AI | A limited, tested AI feature such as read-only diagnostics |
| Platform | Kubernetes, self-service templates, golden paths and developer experience |

Each module can include its own **Practical Assessment**; these feed into the larger Final Project. Keep your chosen project reproducible and document the important design decisions. Sample data and non-confidential environments are sufficient.

## 4. Track real progress

A simple log is enough:

| Module / activity | Hours spent | Learning / lab completed | Evidence link | Next step |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | |

Consider a module complete when you can demonstrate its objectives and practical tasks. Use **working evidence**, not time spent, as the measure of progress.

## 5. Add optional certifications

Certifications can help validate skills, but they are **not required** to complete the programme. Select exams that match your goals, check current provider requirements, and schedule preparation alongside—rather than instead of—the practical work.

## 6. Adapt without losing the foundation

Adjust dates, pace, optional credentials and areas of deeper specialisation to fit your needs. Keep track of any topics you omit or replace.
